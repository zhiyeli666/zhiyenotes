### ✍️ Reflection

When a holiday shortens the trading week, the news and other market-moving events that would normally be spread across five trading days are compressed into four, so each day has to absorb more, and that amplifies volatility.

The Fed pays more attention to the number that strips out food and energy when it judges inflation, because food and energy prices are mostly determined by other factors, such as war, rather than by inflation itself.
