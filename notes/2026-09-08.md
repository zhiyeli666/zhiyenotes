### ✍️ Reflection

The Dow fell much more than the Nasdaq, and the reason was oil rather than interest rates. The Dow holds more industrial, transport and consumer companies, and their costs rise directly when oil rises. The Nasdaq is mostly software and chip companies, for which fuel is only a small expense. Two large chip stocks, Intel and Qualcomm, also jumped on their own news that day, which held the Nasdaq up.

Expensive oil pushes prices up across the economy, because almost everything has to be moved and made. The Fed cannot produce more oil, but it can raise rates to stop the energy spike from spreading into other prices and into what people expect inflation to be later, so a hike becomes more likely.
