Research Note · No. 13

A $104 Billion Loss, Mostly on Paper

Anthropic, PBC reported a net loss of $104,039 million for the nine months ended September 30, 2026. It then closed an initial public offering and a concurrent private placement. Class A shares, ticker ANT, were priced at $83.00 and began trading on the Nasdaq Global Select Market on November 19. On November 20, Anthropic said the offering, including the full exercise of the underwriters' option, had aggregate gross proceeds of about $93.5 billion, of which about $91.6 billion went to the company, and that a private placement of Class N shares added about $26.0 billion, for about $119.5 billion before discounts and expenses. This note uses the final prospectus, dated November 18, 2026 and filed November 19, and Anthropic's own pricing and closing announcements. It does not treat a noncash charge as economically meaningless.

1. Where the Loss Came From

Most of the loss was not recorded in the operating business. Revenue was $33,242 million, operating expenses were $34,502 million, and the operating loss was $1,260 million, about 3.8% of revenue derived. Interest showed up as income of $2,359 million. The statements of operations do not present a separate interest-expense line.

A $94,324 million charge for the change in fair value of convertible notes, derivative liabilities, and simple agreements for future equity was about 91% of the net loss derived. The prospectus says it was driven primarily by an increase in the fair value of outstanding convertible promissory notes as enterprise valuation rose. Other income (expense), net was an expense of $10,397 million, of which $11,008 million was the fair value of partner financing arrangements. After tax, net loss was $104,039 million, against $25,107 million in the same nine months of 2025. Those two fair-value items together were $105,332 million derived — more than the net loss — and both are added back in the cash-flow statement. They did not, in themselves, use cash, but they are still real. The company will keep marking remaining convertible notes to market until they are converted, repaid, or settled. After the conversions assumed in the prospectus, convertible notes payable were still $109,742 million. When those notes convert, the cost shows up as shares.

2. What Running Claude Actually Costs

The company no longer presents gross profit on the face of the income statement. In 2025 it switched from a functional presentation to expenses by nature. Compute and infrastructure, $18,808 million, was the largest line, about 56.6% of revenue derived. Personnel was $4,863 million, including $3.3 billion of stock-based compensation. Sales, marketing, and partnerships were $5,529 million. Other operating and administrative expenses were $5,302 million.

A supplemental note still groups the same $34,502 million by function, including cost of revenue of $4,812 million and research and development of $19,240 million. That would imply gross profit of $28,430 million, or about 85.5% of sales derived. This note does not treat that figure as the cost of running Claude: substantially all of cost of revenue is compute used to serve customers, and research and development is primarily more compute for model development, plus people and overhead. The cleaner reading is the $18,808 million compute line.

Revenue was about 1,206% above the $2,545 million recorded in the same stretch of 2025 derived. The operating loss narrowed from $5,224 million to $1,260 million. Run-Rate Revenue was approximately $81 billion as of October 31, 2026, up $10 billion from September 30 — an operating metric, not GAAP revenue and not a forecast.

3. Follow the Cash

Operating activities provided $6,569 million, after having used $2,024 million a year earlier. The bridge from a $104,039 million net loss is mostly the fair-value add-backs, plus $3,255 million of stock-based compensation and other noncash items, partly offset by working-capital uses, including a $7,076 million increase in accounts receivable.

Investing activities used $36,546 million, mostly the short-term investment portfolio. Cash spent on property and equipment was $925 million; prepayments for leased assets and equipment were $1,955 million. Those spent amounts are not the future compute bill. As of September 30, 2026, expected future fixed or minimum guaranteed purchase commitments, primarily cloud hosting and other infrastructure, were approximately $313.8 billion. Agreements without a fixed minimum are excluded and described as expected to be significant. Separately, leases not yet commenced carried undiscounted future obligations of $156.2 billion, and after September 30 the company added new and amended arrangements of approximately $106 billion, some contingent, plus short-term minimums of approximately $7 billion. The filing does not present these as one combined total, so this note does not add them.

Financing activities provided $110,735 million, almost entirely from issuing preferred stock. Cash, cash equivalents, and short-term investments were $131,845 million as of September 30, or $244.6 billion pro forma as adjusted if the underwriters' option was exercised in full — which it was. The prospectus estimated net proceeds to Anthropic of approximately $117.0 billion in that case. The November 20 close reported only gross proceeds.

4. What Shareholders Are Buying

At the $83.00 IPO price, and if the underwriters' option was exercised in full, the prospectus shows 7,730,103,996 Class A shares and 4,789,697,318 Class N shares outstanding on a pro forma as adjusted basis as of September 30, 2026. Class N has the same economic rights as Class A; Class T and Class F have none. Excluding subsidiary-held Class N shares, which are not outstanding for accounting purposes, the economic shares the prospectus treats as outstanding are 12,519,801,314 derived. At $83.00, that is an equity value of about $1.04 trillion derived — about 31 times nine-month revenue derived, not a full-year multiple, or about 13 times the $81 billion run-rate figure derived.

That count already includes the preferred-stock conversion, the conversion of some convertible notes into 292,768,653 shares, the 1,103,351,354 Class A shares Anthropic sold once the option was exercised, and the 313,253,007 Class N shares issued in the private placement. It does not include 1,774,342,693 option shares at a weighted-average exercise price of $2.91, 1,026,660,010 RSU shares still unvested as of November 19, 2026, or 1,547,676,309 shares issuable later on remaining convertible notes that could not convert at the offering without exceeding a holder's ownership cap. Those notes are the $109,742 million still on the pro forma balance sheet. Conversion is dilution.

5. Bottom Line

The $104,039 million net loss is in the company's own prospectus. Most of it is not the cash cost of running Claude in that period. The operating loss was $1,260 million, compute cost $18,808 million, and operating activities provided $6,569 million of cash. About 91% of the net loss derived was a fair-value charge recorded as the enterprise became more valuable — claims that convert into shares, or that remain outstanding and continue to be marked to market.

Shareholders at $83.00 are paying about $1.04 trillion derived for a business that was still slightly loss-making at the operating line, with hundreds of billions of dollars of remaining compute commitments still ahead of it. The cash raised on November 20 is what funds those commitments. The $104,039 million is not that bill.
