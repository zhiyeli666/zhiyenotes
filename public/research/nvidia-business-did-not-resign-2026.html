Research Note · No. 12

The Business Did Not Resign

Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia's president, chief executive and a director on November 12, effective immediately. The stock moved, and it had already been moving. The company's last published accounts did not. This note separates what has changed at the top from what, as of today, has not yet shown up in the results.

Zhiye's Notes · November 15, 2026 · Last reported results through Q2 FY2027; CEO transition as of November 12; Q3 results due November 17

What left the company

Nvidia's board announced on November 12 that Huang had resigned from all three roles, effective immediately. Colette Kress, chief financial officer since 2013, was appointed interim chief executive and will keep the finance job until a permanent successor is named. The board is searching inside and outside the company, and named longtime director Harvey C. Jones as its first chair. Kress already presented the August results and is scheduled to present the third quarter on November 17; the earnings date was not moved. No permanent successor was named. The board said it expected operations, customer commitments and the product roadmap to continue without interruption — a statement of intent, not a result.

What the stock already did

The shares were falling before the resignation, and they fell again when it was announced. How far depends on the window, and the published figures do not agree.

On November 5, more than a week before Huang left, 24/7 Wall St. reported that Nvidia closed at $186.24, down 6.94% on the day and down 22.04% over the prior month.

On November 12, NBC News wrote that Nvidia fell as much as 16% during the session and closed down 4.3%, and that reports surrounding Huang had erased about $500 billion in market value. TechCrunch, the same day, said the shares were down nearly 12% in early morning trading, and that since the previous Friday the stock had fallen as much as 19%, with a market-cap loss of more than $855 billion. Those are different snapshots, and this note does not fold them into one total. A move in the stock is not, by itself, evidence that the business has changed.

Then the quarter

The last complete accounts are still the second quarter of fiscal 2027, reported on August 26 for the period ended July 26, 2026. Third-quarter actuals are due on November 17. In the second quarter, Data Center was about 92.5% of revenue derived, and GAAP operating income was about 66.2% of sales derived. The third-quarter revenue midpoint is about 12.3% above the second-quarter total derived. The company said it was not assuming any Data Center compute revenue from China.

On the August 26 call, Kress said the company expected to grow revenue by about 70% in fiscal 2028, and called that a "supply-constrained outlook." Huang, then still chief executive, said the unconstrained outlook "would be a lot higher." That longer view has not been updated.

What the accounts cannot show

A stock can move for reasons that do not appear in the last income statement: a founder leaving, a successor still to be found, or questions about how customers and governments will respond. Those questions may matter later. They are not a substitute for the quarter, which is the first place they can start to appear in Nvidia's own words.

What to watch after this

Three things will say more than the resignation did: whether the quarter and the commentary still match the August outlook, including supply as the limit on growth; the new fourth-quarter guide, and any update to the fiscal 2028 view; and the search for a permanent chief executive. The first report under Kress as interim CEO is on November 17. The first full period after Huang's departure still lies ahead.

Bottom line

As of today, the business has not been shown to have changed.

The founder has left. Kress is running the company on an interim basis and will still present the accounts. The stock has already reacted, before the announcement and on the day itself, by amounts that differ from one report to the next. On the page, the August quarter and the $108.0 billion third-quarter revenue guide, plus or minus 2%, are still the figures the company has given, along with the call's description of about 70% growth in fiscal 2028, limited by supply.

Whether that still describes the company depends on the November 17 report — the first accounts that can confirm the August guide, miss it, or replace it. Until then, the founder has left; the business, in the only accounts we have, has not been shown to have left with him.
