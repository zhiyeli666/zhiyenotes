Research Note · No. 2

Losing Share, Raising Prices

Google, Amazon, Microsoft and Meta are all making their own AI chips, and it is working. Nvidia just had its biggest quarter ever — and says it still cannot build chips fast enough.

Zhiye's Market Notes · August 29, 2026 · Data through Q2 FY2027

Verdict

Custom chips really are taking market share. It has not cost Nvidia anything yet, and the proof is the price.

Chips built by the cloud companies are now about 28% of all AI servers sold, up from almost nothing three years ago. They are growing 44.6% a year, against 16.1% for GPUs. That part of the story is true.

But in the same week, Nvidia reported sales of $96.2 billion, up 106%. Data centre sales rose 117%. Gross margin was 75.0%. It told investors to expect $108 billion next quarter. And it said next year's growth — about 70% — is limited by how much it can make, not by how much it can sell. It is also putting prices up.

That last part settles it. A company losing a price war cuts prices. Nvidia is raising them, at a 75% margin, while it runs short of supply. Whatever the custom chips are taking, they are not taking Nvidia's power to set prices. Until they do, losing share is just a number.

1. The claim, written so it can be wrong

The case against Nvidia is simple, and it sounds right. Its biggest customers are also the companies best able to compete with it. Google has TPUs. Amazon has Trainium. Microsoft has Maia. Meta has MTIA. Every one of those is a chip Nvidia does not get to sell.

The claim. As the cloud companies build their own chips, they buy fewer from Nvidia. Nvidia loses market share, then loses the power to set prices, then loses growth.

That is three steps, and they have to happen in that order. You cannot lose sales to a rival who has not first taken share. So the fair way to test it is to check each step, and see how far reality has actually got.

2. Step one — are custom chips really taking share?

Yes. This part is simply true, and worth saying plainly before the rest of the note complicates it.

TrendForce counts custom chips at 27.8% of AI servers sold in 2026 — the highest since 2023 — with GPUs at 69.7%. Those custom chips are growing 44.6% this year, against 16.1% for GPUs: nearly three times as fast. Amazon says it has put more than a million Trainium chips to work, and calls it a business worth billions.

So step one holds. Nvidia used to have almost the whole market and now has about three quarters of it. Anyone who said in 2024 that the cloud companies would never get their own chips working was wrong.

3. Step two — machines are not money

Here the story starts to fall apart, on a difference most headlines skip.

That 27.8% counts machines sold. Counted in money, custom chips are only about 16%. Both numbers are right, and the gap between them is the point. A custom chip is a cheaper machine than a rack of Nvidia's Blackwells. The cloud companies build their own chips because they are cheaper — so every sale they win is worth less than the sale Nvidia lost.

The size comparison makes it plainer. Nvidia's data centre sales went from about $41 billion a year ago to $89.0 billion this quarter derived — a rise of about $48 billion in one quarter. If custom chips are 16% of a market worth roughly $122 billion a quarter derived, then all of them together earn about $20 billion a quarter. Nvidia's growth is more than twice the size of the whole thing that is supposed to be eating it.

4. Step three — the price test

A company can lose share slowly and safely for years. What tells you a rival is really hurting you is the price. So this is the step that matters, and it is where the claim breaks.

Gross margin is 75.0% now, guided to 74.0% next quarter, then 71–72%, then back to about 72–73%. A falling margin sounds exactly like competition biting. Which is why the reason for it matters so much.

This is the whole argument in one sentence. The margin is falling because memory got expensive, not because Nvidia is cutting prices to keep customers — and the plan to fix it is to charge more. You cannot raise prices in a market where customers have a cheaper option they are willing to take.

The supply comment says the same thing. The finance chief put next year's growth near 70% and said the limit was how much Nvidia can build. A company losing customers has spare capacity. A company running short has the opposite problem. Both things were said on the same call, and only one of them fits the claim.

5. Why both things are true at once

The mistake is treating market share like a fixed pie. It is not. The cloud companies did not move their Nvidia orders over to their own chips. They added a huge amount of new capacity, filled some of it with their own chips, and kept buying every Nvidia chip they could get. Custom chips growing 44.6% while Nvidia grows over 100% means Nvidia is gaining in dollars while losing in percent.

So the thing worth watching is not Trainium or TPU. It is the cloud companies spending less on data centres, because that is the only way losing share and losing sales become the same thing. And the first sign that custom chips have started to bite will not be a market share headline. It will be Nvidia cutting prices.
