Research Note · No. 8

Losing Screen Time, Not Money

Netflix's share price has fallen by a quarter this year, and the reason everyone gives is YouTube: people are spending more of their TV time there and less on Netflix. The first half is true. But Netflix's own accounts show no damage at all — it is making more money from every hour people watch.

Zhiye's Notes · October 17, 2026 · Company figures through June 30, 2026

Verdict

False. YouTube is winning the fight for TV time, but losing that fight has not hurt Netflix's business. Its sales and profits are growing seven times faster than its viewing.

In a year, Netflix's share of all American TV viewing slipped from 8.8% to 7.8% while YouTube's climbed to a record 14.2%. That part of the story is real.

Over the same stretch, Netflix's revenue grew 14.7% and its operating profit 14.4%, comparing the first half of 2026 with the first half of 2025 derived. Hours watched grew just 2%. The difference is that each hour watched now brings in 25.6¢, up from 22.8¢ derived.

1. The claim, written so it can be wrong

Netflix had a rough September. The stock fell 14% in the month. Two big banks cut their ratings, and both pointed to the same thing: YouTube taking a bigger share of the time people spend in front of the TV. Even Netflix's co-CEO said at an event that "we're not growing as fast as I want us to."

The claim. YouTube is eating Netflix. People are moving their TV time to YouTube, and that is starting to damage Netflix's business.

The claim has two halves, and they can be checked separately:

  • Prediction 1: Netflix is losing TV time to YouTube.
  • Prediction 2: this is showing up in Netflix's sales.
  • Prediction 3: this is showing up in Netflix's profits.

2. Is Netflix losing TV time? Yes

The research firm Nielsen measures what share of all TV viewing in American homes goes to each company — every channel, every app, on the TV set. Here is July 2025 against July 2026.

YouTube gained 0.8 points and Netflix lost 1.0. The direction is clear: YouTube's share is higher than a year ago and Netflix's is lower. Prediction 1 is true.

Two details are worth keeping in mind, though. First, Netflix's own viewing is not shrinking. Its members watched more than 97 billion hours in the first half of 2026, up 2% on the year before. Netflix is not losing hours; it is just growing more slowly than YouTube. Second, the biggest loser on the chart is not Netflix but cable TV, which dropped 3.5 points derived — more than YouTube's gain and Netflix's loss put together.

3. Is it hurting sales? No

If YouTube were eating Netflix, the first place it would show is in how much money Netflix takes in. It does not show.

That is not a forecast holding up on hope. The first half of 2026 has already happened: revenue was $24.81 billion, against $21.62 billion in the first half of 2025 — up 14.7% derived. That is the year when Netflix's share of TV time was supposedly being eaten. Prediction 2 is false.

4. Is it hurting profits? No

Profits tell the same story. Operating profit — what is left from sales after paying for shows, staff and everything else that runs the business — was $8.15 billion in the first half of 2026, up from $7.12 billion a year earlier. That is +14.4% derived, almost exactly in line with sales.

The margin, the share of each dollar of sales kept as operating profit, has climbed every year: 26.7% in 2024, 29.5% in 2025, and 31.5% forecast for 2026. The second quarter of 2026 was a little lower than a year earlier, 33.4% against 34.1%, but Netflix had told investors in April to expect that, because more of this year's new shows were released in the first half. It expects 33.2% in the third quarter, against 28.2% a year ago. Prediction 3 is false.

5. How you lose time and still make more money

The trick is in one number: how much Netflix earns for every hour someone watches it.

Hours went up 2%. Money went up about 15%. So each hour watched now brings in about 12% more than it did a year ago. Netflix's letters give two reasons:

  • Higher prices. Netflix raised prices in the first half of 2026 in markets including the US, Mexico and Spain, and said the results were in line with its earlier price rises and with what it expected.
  • Advertising. The cheaper plan with ads is now a real business. Ad revenue went from over $1.5 billion in 2025 to an expected $3 billion this year — roughly doubling.

Think of a cinema whose share of the town's free time slips a little because a new park opens. If the cinema still sells as many tickets as before, charges a bit more for each one, and starts showing adverts before the film, it makes more money than ever. The park winning does not mean the cinema is losing.

6. What the three answers add up to

Only one of the three predictions held. Netflix is losing share of TV time to YouTube — that is real and measurable. But the claim that this is eating Netflix's business fails twice. Sales grew 14.7% in the first half of 2026. Profits grew 14.4%. Each hour of viewing earned 12% more than the year before.

The share price, down 25% this year and 43% from its high last October, is pricing in the claim. The accounts are not showing it. A company whose viewing grows 2% and whose revenue grows 15% has turned slow viewing growth into fast money growth. That is not what being eaten looks like.
